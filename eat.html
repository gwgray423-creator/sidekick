Why you can't decide what to eat

It isn't a hard decision. It's a low-stakes decision with too many options — and that's a different kind of trap.

When every option is roughly fine, comparing them has no payoff. There's no clearly right answer to find, so your brain keeps re-opening the same shortlist instead of closing it. That's why "I don't care, you pick" turns into twenty minutes of nothing. The fix isn't a better list. It's an ending.

Three things that actually end it

1. Cut the list to three. More than three options and the comparison cost outweighs the benefit. Any more than that is noise wearing a menu.

2. Pick before you look. Choose a cuisine first, then a dish. Deciding at the wrong level is what keeps the loop open.

3. Accept "good enough." The gap between a great dinner and a fine dinner is small. The gap between a fine dinner and no dinner is not.
